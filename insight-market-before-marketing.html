Map the market before you touch the marketing.

SOSheridan Orr

Most marketing teams start with leads or brand. They pick channels, write slogans and launch campaigns, then wonder why pipeline doesn't convert. The missing step comes before all of that: understanding the market you're selling into.

Start with the forces, not the funnel

Every buyer's decision sits inside a larger picture. There are the players competing for the same budget, the dynamics shifting the category, and the headwinds and tailwinds shaping what buyers can and can't do this year. A market map lays all of that out before a single headline gets written.

When you can see those forces, you stop guessing about what matters to buyers. You know which conversations are already happening in their world, and you can join them.

You can't build great marketing without being a student of markets.

Ask what's really driving the decision

B2B buyers rarely decide on features alone. A purchase might be driven by fear of falling behind, by internal politics or by a trend no one in the room can control. Those motives shape what a buyer needs to hear, and from whom.

David Ogilvy said you can't be a good copywriter without being a student of human nature. The same logic applies one level up. Great marketing comes from studying the market as closely as the buyer.

Look at competitors the way they look at you

Your competitors are making moves, and so are the substitutes buyers weigh alongside you, including doing nothing. Applying game theory as a lens helps you anticipate how the field will respond, which positions are crowded and where there's room to win.

Try this: list the four or five options a buyer considers alongside you, including "keep doing what we do today." For each one, write the single reason a buyer might choose it over you. Your message has to answer every one of those reasons.

Why it matters more now

AI has created traffic jams in every channel. There is more content, more outreach and more noise than buyers can process. In that environment, story matters more than ever, and a real story comes from understanding the market and the buyer deeply enough to say something that lands.

That's the system: understand the market, sharpen your ideal customer, write the message, and the right leads, pipeline and revenue follow. Strategy is what fuels it.
