Her story

Valuation first. Marketing second.

Sheridan started her career in corporate development, so she learned how companies are valued before she learned how they're marketed.

That order still shapes every engagement. Before writing a headline or approving a campaign, she asks what a buyer, a board or an investor will conclude from a company's story, and whether the evidence supports it.

Her method begins with the market itself: the players, the dynamics, the headwinds and tailwinds, and the forces shaping each buyer's world. What's driving the decision? Fear, politics, a trend nobody can control? She looks at the competitive field through the lens of game theory, because that analysis separates a message that connects from one that adds to the noise.

David Ogilvy said you can't be a good copywriter without being a student of human nature. Sheridan extends that idea: you can't build great marketing without being a student of markets. With AI crowding every channel, that work matters more than ever. A real story comes from understanding the market and the buyer well enough to say something that lands.

She also studied medieval and early modern literature at the graduate level, writing her thesis on power and persuasion in Shakespeare's tragedies. It turned out to be excellent training for understanding why people believe what they believe.
